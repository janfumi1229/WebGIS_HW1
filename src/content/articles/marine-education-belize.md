These 2 week, I had the incredible opportunity to facilitate environmental education sessions for Standard 5 & 6 students. This was my very first time teaching abroad!

As a core member of the NTU Climate Action Club, I’ve often shared environmental insights with university peers. However, translating those complex concepts into engaging, digestible lessons for primary school students was a completely new and rewarding challenge.

Since both Taiwan and Belize are deeply tied to the ocean and rivers through culture and daily life, I centered the curriculum on the theme: "Our Rivers, Our Oceans."

Our mission was simple: to plant the seeds of eco-conscious action in young hearts and foster an exchange of perspectives from different corners of the world.

In our first session, we explored Taiwan's cultural and natural landscapes alongside Belize's unique aquatic wonders. Living along the New River, the community of Orange Walk shares a profound bond with its freshwater ecosystem. We discussed the severe 2019 eutrophication event, where algae overgrowth oxygen-deprived the river and harmed local aquatic life. That crisis mobilized the community, inspiring annual riverbank cleanups by schools and volunteers on Service Day ever since.

Building on local context, we zoomed out to the global marine debris crisis, examining solid waste management challenges in Belize and around the world. To empower students with practical solutions, we discussed everyday actions they can take:
♻️ Practicing the 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle)
🥤 Bringing reusable utensils, water bottles, and bags
🧹 Participating in local river and beach cleanups
📢 Spreading environmental awareness among friends and family

To close the session, every student wrote their own "Ocean Pledge"! A personal commitment to a small, daily action for our planet. We displayed these pledges as a collective "Action Board" to serve as a visual reminder of their commitments.

Teaching these vibrant young minds reminded me that environmental education isn't just about sharing facts, it's about building empathy and agency. Seeing the kids eagerly write down their ocean pledges gave me so much hope. Big changes truly start with small, everyday actions, and I'm deeply grateful to play a part in their sustainability journey here in Belize!
